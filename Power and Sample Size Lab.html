Power and Sample Size Lab

Power is the probability that an experiment rejects the null hypothesis when a specific true difference exists. It is set by four inputs: sample size, the size of the true difference, the variability of the outcome, and the significance level. Move them and watch the two sampling distributions slide, overlap and separate.

What power does and does not tell you

Power belongs to an assumed effect

"80% power" means nothing until a true difference is attached to it. Use the smallest difference worth detecting, not the difference you hope to find.

Observed power adds nothing

Power computed from the estimate you just obtained is a one-to-one function of the p-value. After the data are in, report the confidence interval instead.

A null result from a weak design is not evidence of no effect

At 50% power, half of real effects of the assumed size go undetected. Failing to reject H₀ is not the same as accepting it.

Low power also exaggerates the effects that do reach significance

Run the simulation with a low-powered design: significant experiments overestimate Δ on average, because only large chance deviations clear the threshold. This is a Type M (magnitude) error.

α is not the chance that a significant finding is false

It is the false-positive rate when H₀ is true. The share of significant results that are false also depends on power and on how many of the tested hypotheses are truly null.

The inputs are estimates

A σ from a small pilot or a single paper is itself uncertain. Move σ and Δ here to see how the required n shifts, and plan with a conservative σ. Binary, survival, clustered or repeated-measures outcomes need different formulas.

Method: Student two-sample t-test, equal n and equal variances, normally distributed outcomes. Power is computed exactly from the noncentral t distribution by numerical integration, not by the normal approximation. The curves above are the t distributions rescaled by the standard error σ√(2/n), so the horizontal axis is the observed difference in measurement units and the second axis row gives the matching t. In a real experiment the SD is estimated from the data, so the cutoff in raw units varies slightly between runs.
